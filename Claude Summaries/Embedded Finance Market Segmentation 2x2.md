# Embedded Finance Market Segmentation 2x2
## Context
Karl is building a strategy 2x2 of embedded finance use cases, meaning programs where Green Dot provides an account or card. The axes are funding certainty (y) and feature-set sophistication (x). The goal is to show where Green Dot's integrated bundle wins and to use that for sales strategy. The work moved from a PowerPoint draft to Karl's own revised chart, then to a whiteboard-style explainer video with a narration script. ## Key findings
- **Scope:** embedded account and card programs only. Cash in/out (GDN) is excluded.
- **Merged segments:**
  - Closed-loop spend = retail wallets + unattended stored value + vertical SaaS consumer programs
  - Branded debit = 1st-party + affinity
  - Gig payouts split between payroll cards (top-left) and EWA/instant pay (top-right)
- **Quadrant placements:**
  - Closed-loop is "complex" because it needs a POS acceptance integration or transaction-level spend controls. Open loop is easier because it rides the card networks.
  - Remittance is top-left, scoped to domestic funding.
  - Brokerage/crypto is high on funding certainty because the target customers already hold balances at the brokerage.
  - BNPL/consumer lending stays top-right.
  - Unrestricted government payouts go in "benefit payouts" (top-left). Restricted benefits and HSA/FSA go top-right.
  - Incentive cards sit with settlements and payouts.
  - SMB debit was added to the expense management box. TikTok merchant payouts sit under marketplace payouts.
- **Color meaning:** green = Green Dot is already a significant player. Green segments include SBTPG (tax refunds), Rapid! (payroll), FSC, and wallets/P2P (Apple Cash and Samsung). Yellow = not currently served.
- **Insights:**
  - Three of the six target-quadrant (top-left) segments are already green, which suggests a core-adjacency growth path.
  - The bottom-left quadrant is nearly empty. That fits Karl's view that simple programs which must earn deposits (like branded debit) haven't been a large market.
  - Most GDN partners (Venmo, PayPal, Chime, Varo, Dave, Robinhood, Branch, DoorDash) fall on the right side. That points to "layer plays" (bank issuing or the cash network) rather than selling the bundle.
  - Apple Cash and Samsung prove Green Dot's strength with wallets, but Karl says we haven't figured out how to sell it. Check with Legal and Marketing what can be referenced publicly.
- **Quadrant narratives (Karl's wording):**
  - **Bottom-left:** branded/prepaid programs for non-fintech brands. Simple needs, but they must build a compelling case to attract deposits.
  - **Top-left:** Green Dot's sweet spot (SBTPG, FSC, Rapid!). These customers are already in the funds flow.
  - **Bottom-right:** sophisticated fintechs that embed funds flows and spend controls.
  - **Top-right:** mature fintechs that want composable processor, bank and BaaS solutions.

## Decisions
- Plot use cases, not partner types.
- Keep "funding certainty" as the y-axis for now. Stocks vs. flows analysis is deferred to the next pass.
- "Complexity" means complexity of what the use case requires (still debating whether that means Green Dot's slice or the use case overall).
- Karl will narrate the video himself. The sandbox has no text-to-speech; the macOS `say` command is an option.
